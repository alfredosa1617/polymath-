Trachtenberg Multiplication by 11 Rule

1. The last number of the multiplicand becomes the last digit of the answer.
2. Each successive digit of the multiplicand is added to its right-hand neighbor.
3. The first digit of the multiplicand becomes the first digit of the answer (plus any carry).

The 5 Parameters of ASL

Linguistic Anatomy
1. Handshape (Designator / DEZ)

Flat open B-hand, four fingers touching together with thumb relaxed beside palm.

2. Location (Tabula / TAB)

Fingertips gently touch the center of your chin or lower lip.

3. Movement (Sig / SIG)

Move your hand smoothly forward and slightly downward toward the conversational partner.

4. Palm Orientation (ORI)

Palm facing inward toward yourself at the start, tilting slightly upward as it moves forward.

5. Facial Expression & Non-Manual Posture

Pleasant, friendly facial expression with a gentle smile and relaxed, open posture.

Regional & Dialect Variation

In casual conversation, one hand is standard. To express profound gratitude, both hands can be used symmetrically.

Sociolinguistics Historical Dialect

Black American Sign Language (BASL)

Evolved during racial segregation in the American South when Black Deaf students were educated in separate institutions. BASL features distinct lexical items, a broader signing space, two-handed variants of standard one-handed signs, and expressive cadence.

Key Linguistic Marker: Preservation of older forms of ASL signs that modern mainstream ASL has reduced or consolidated.
Grammar Non-Manuals

Facial Grammar & Body Posture

In ASL, eyebrows, head tilts, and mouth morphemes are not optional emotion modifiers; they serve as primary syntactic grammar:

  • Raised Eyebrows: Signals a Yes/No question or conditional clause.
  • Furrowed Eyebrows: Signals a WH-question (Who, What, Where, When, Why).
  • Head Nod/Shake: Grammatical negation or affirmation.
Regionality Geography

Geographic Lexical Shifts

Just like spoken English accents and vocabulary (e.g. "soda" vs "pop"), ASL features regional variations across states and communities for common words such as "BIRTHDAY", "HOSPITAL", "STRAWBERRY", and "PICNIC".

Sage's Teaching Principle: Sage teaches standard generalized ASL while explicitly annotating regional variants so you communicate fluently in any Deaf community.
